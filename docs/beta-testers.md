# BETA TESTERS WANTED

Hi everyone! I’ve wanted a PicoBrew since the Kickstarter days and finally have a C, S, Pro and Zymatic of my own. Thanks, Dave Ebel! I’m a software engineer, and I’ve been using AI to help me reverse engineer the firmware and build a way to brew with these machines on a home network.

There are two parts you can use together or separately. The custom firmware gives your machine a local website and an API. On a Pico S, Pro or C, for example, you can open the brewer in a browser, see its actual display, turn or press the dial, manage saved recipes and review past brews. You can brew with the Hub turned off.

The optional PicoBrew Hub runs on Mac, Windows, Linux or Raspberry Pi. Plug your machine into the Hub computer, open **Brewery → Add machine → USB setup and recovery**, and it walks you through backing up the original firmware and installing the new one. Keep that backup if you want to go back to stock. Once provisioned, S, Pro, C, compatible Z modules, PicoStill and PicoFerm can get later updates over Wi-Fi. The Zymatic uses USB for firmware updates.

The Hub lets you build recipes around the grain and hop slots your brewer actually has, browse community recipes, send a recipe to a machine, and follow multiple brews on one screen. The journal puts temperatures, step changes and your own notes on the same timeline. You can plan fermentation and carbonation, attach PicoFerm readings to a batch, and manage a PicoStill. An optional AI assistant can help draft a recipe, talk through a live brew or review a finished one. You decide what to change.

You can try parts of it on factory firmware, too. A stock Zymatic on 1.1.14 can fetch recipes and report sessions to the Hub. A stock Z on 0.0.119 can import a Hub recipe through Z Program Maker. The project README lists the controls and update options available for each model.

I’d especially like to hear from people willing to test with their own machine. Tell me which model you have and what you tried, whether that’s getting connected for the first time, loading a favorite recipe or following a brew. I’m happy to jump on a Google Meet to walk through setup or hear what could be better.
